Seven years. Hugo, Ghost, WordPress, Jekyll, Astro, Grav -- the full tour.
Every one of them taught me something, mostly about what I don't want.

What I want is this: words in a repository, pushed to a remote, rendered by a
machine I do not have to think about. So this site runs on a frozen Hugo
setup. Same version locally and in CI. A theme I own outright, small enough to
read in one sitting. No upgrades scheduled, ever.

The rules, recorded publicly so I can't renegotiate them with myself at 1 a.m.:

1. If it's not in the repo, it doesn't exist. Other apps are buffers with an
   expiry date.
2. Posts are finished-ish. Notes are the labeled junk drawer -- unfinished is
   legal there.
3. Files are organized by date. Topics are tags. One physical location, many
   labels, zero duplication.
4. The `draft` flag is the publish switch. Nothing else gates shipping.

And the freeze: no theme work, no Hugo upgrades, no "just one tweak" for six
months. Writing, publishing, tagging, linking -- those are allowed. Everything
else waits until April.

This post is the signature on the declaration. The press is built. Now the
only remaining job is to use it.
